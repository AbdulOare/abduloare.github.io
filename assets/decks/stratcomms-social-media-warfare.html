The Reframe · Four Rules of the Ground

Terrain,

A channel is something you switch on when you have news. Terrain is where you live. Nobody opts out of terrain. You hold it, or you lose it.
01 · PRESENCE
You are on it whether you post or not
Your institution is discussed, mocked, defended and impersonated online every day. Absence does not remove you from the terrain. It only removes your voice from it.

The Battlefield · The Attention Economy
05 / 30

The feed is an auction. Accuracy is not a bid.

What the algorithm actually rewards
01
Emotion: outrage and fear outperform accuracy on every engagement metric.
02
Speed: the first version of a story earns the reach; corrections inherit the leftovers.
03
Conflict: a fight retains attention; a clarification releases it. The feed prefers the fight.
04
Novelty: falsehood is, by construction, newer than the truth. That is its structural advantage.
6×
The speed problem
False news reaches 1,500 people about six times faster than true news: spread by people, not bots (MIT, 126,000 story cascades). Your correction races a six-length head start.
The WhatsApp loop. Nigeria's front line
Closed groups. No public metrics, no community notes, no reply column where truth can fight back. Credibility is borrowed from the sender, not the source: it came from your uncle, so it must be true.
The trust deficit
Institutional voices start behind. Years of “no comment” mean an official denial can confirm a rumour for some audiences. That deficit is repaid in peacetime, never during the crisis.
The algorithm is not against you. It is indifferent, and on this terrain, indifference is worse.

Threats · Deepfakes & Synthetic Media
10 / 30

You will not out-stare a deepfake.
You out-process it.

Capability · Today, not someday
30 seconds of audio clones a voice
Free tools produce a leader's voice “ordering” anything: good enough for a phone speaker in a WhatsApp group, which is where it will be heard. Video passes at feed-scroll speed.
Detection · A losing race, run differently
Provenance beats pixels
Artefacts (lip-sync drift, studio-perfect silence, warped hands) catch cheap fakes only. The durable questions: who posted it first, when, and can anyone show where it has been since it was recorded? A one-day-old account is a louder tell than any pixel.
Pre-emption · Decided in peacetime
Make fakes break your pattern
Publish the list of channels you actually announce on. Archive genuine footage of your leaders. Agree a callback protocol: any voice instruction arriving outside the agreed channel is verified on the agreed channel. Every time, whoever's voice it is.
The deepfake's real target is not one message. It is the day your genuine footage gets dismissed as fake. Defend that day now.
Threats · Personal Accounts
11 / 30

Your official account has a firewall.
Your personal account has your ego.

Four openings the adversary uses
01
The public org chart: LinkedIn plus Facebook rebuilds your structure, postings and reporting lines in an afternoon.
02
The location-tagged photo: a phone photo quietly records exactly where it was taken; the background shows your layout.
03
The friendly stranger: the recruiter, the admirer, the “journalist”. Six weeks of small talk before the first question that matters.
04
The reused password: one breached shopping site, and a personal breach becomes an institutional breach.
The rule
Your personal account is an official attack surface. The adversary does not distinguish between your two profiles, so from today, neither do you.
The family dimension
Your spouse's check-ins and your children's school posts map your movements as precisely as yours do. The household is the perimeter: brief it like one.
The quiet failure
Most security losses are not leaks. They are accumulations: a hundred innocent posts assembled by a patient analyst into one useful picture.
Nobody posts the secret. Everybody posts the pieces.
Threats · Case Study, Anonymised Composite
12 / 30
One operation, dissected

The operation was weeks old before
its “trigger” ever happened.

The surface story
A checkpoint arrest video “proves” an institution is targeting one community.
The arrest was real and lawful: body-camera footage existed. Within two hours the clip was trending with a communal framing, the institution's own recruitment hashtag was flooded with it, and two influencers posted near-identical threads eleven minutes apart. What looked like a spontaneous eruption was a machine that had been assembled for weeks: waiting for a usable trigger.
Phase 1 · Preparation
Weeks before. Accounts batch-created and aged. Community groups joined. Credibility farmed with football banter and job adverts.
Phase 2 · Trigger
A real, ambiguous event. Operations prefer real triggers: the facts are checkable, the framing is not.
Phase 3 · Amplification
The spend. Bot spike inside 40 minutes, the institution's own hashtag hijacked, paid influencers on schedule.
Phase 4 · Capture
Mainstream pickup. Blogs cite the trend as evidence; radio cites the blogs. The narrative hardens into “what everyone knows”.
The tells were all visible in Phase 1: account age, synchronised phrasing, and a hashtag that pivots. Monitoring exists to catch operations before they spend.

Defence · The Response Decision Tree
15 / 30
Click each tile to reveal · This tree runs the simulation debrief

Five postures. Only five.

The common error is not the wrong response. It is the right response at the wrong tier.
R1
Ignore
When: fringe account, no traction, engaging = amplifying. A decision with a name and a review time, not an absence of one. Log it, set a check-back, move on.
R2
Monitor
When: rising but unverified. Set tripwires: resharing speed, pickup by verified accounts, press calls, spillover into the streets. Cross one, move tiers. Prepare the correction while you wait.
R3
Correct
When: spreading, false, and yours to answer. Truth sandwich, own channels, trusted messengers. Correct the record, never duel the account that posted it.
R4
Pre-bunk
When: the attack is predictable but has not landed: an operation, an anniversary, a court date. Inoculate the audience against the technique before the content arrives.
R5
Escalate
When: unlawful content, threats, fake audio or video of leaders, or coordinated operations. Evidence pack → platforms, legal, command: in parallel with the public response, never instead of it.
Defence · Speed vs Accuracy
16 / 30

You do not need the full story to speak.
You need three true sentences.

Anatomy of the holding statement
01
We are aware: name the claim's existence, not its content. “We are aware of reports circulating about…”
02
Here is what we are doing: the verifiable action underway. “We are establishing the facts with the unit concerned.”
03
Here is when you will hear from us: a named time. “We will update the public by 16:00 today.” Then keep it.
✕
Never: speculation, blame, denial of what you have not verified, or “no comment”, which the feed translates as “guilty”.
Speed vs accuracy: a false choice
You are not choosing between fast and accurate. You are choosing between what is true now and everything eventually. Say the first. Promise the second.
Own the first hour
Silence is a vacuum, and the terrain abhors one. It fills with speculation within minutes. Pre-approved holding templates turn a 4-hour clearance into a 15-minute one.
Day 1 callback · The Message House
Your roof message is the spine of every holding statement: values first, action second, timeline third. You built it on Day 1: the simulation will make you use it.
Defence · Countering Misinformation
17 / 30

Two clocks: inoculate before,
correct without amplifying after.

Before the attack · Pre-bunking
Warn about the technique, not the content: “expect fake casualty figures within hours of any incident” beats rebutting each figure later. Before the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Western governments published the false stories the attacker was preparing. When those stories appeared, they were already dead.
Small dose plus refutation: inoculation works like a vaccine. Show a weakened example of the lie and take it apart in public.
Publish your pattern: “here is how we announce, and where.” Ukraine's president posted simple nightly phone videos from the same streets; when a fake surrender video came, it broke the pattern and failed in hours.
After the attack · Correction that works
The truth sandwich: fact, brief flag of the falsehood, fact again. Wrong: “NO, we did NOT kill anyone!” Right: “All persons at the checkpoint are safe and accounted for. A false claim is circulating. Footage of the full encounter follows.”
Avoid the repetition trap: never headline the lie. Repetition is how lies become furniture; even your denial rehearses it.
Choose the messenger first: community leaders, veterans, respected local voices. A correction nobody trusts is a second exposure to the lie.
Pre-bunking is cheaper than correction, and correction is cheaper than reputation repair. Spend early.
Defence · Platforms & Practical Listening

Listening posts

Offence · Presence Before You Need It
20 / 30

You cannot open the account during the withdrawal.

The credibility bank
Every helpful, human, truthful post is a deposit. A crisis is a withdrawal. If the first time citizens hear from you is the day of the allegation, you arrive as a stranger asking for credit.
The humanised voice
People follow people. Show faces, name authors, answer replies, admit small imperfections. An account that only issues communiqués is a noticeboard, and nobody defends a noticeboard.
Advocates, with guardrails
Staff, veterans and community voices are messengers people already trust. Empower them with clear rails: share the public, never the operational; disclose the affiliation; when in doubt, ask first.
What good looks like
NCDC · Nigeria
Through COVID-19: plain language, a daily rhythm, and the courage to say “we do not know yet”. The deposit paid out every time a rumour hit.
New Zealand Police · Global
Humour and humanity from a uniformed institution: millions follow it voluntarily. When it speaks seriously, the contrast commands attention.
Ukraine MoD · Under fire
Fast, and fluent in the internet's own language, in an active information war: an institution holding terrain against a state-sized adversary.

Offence · Security Protocols
21 / 30

One hijacked account undoes
five years of deposits.

This slide is doctrine, not advice. Every line is a standing order for both columns.
Official accounts
Two-step login from an app, never by SMS: criminals hijack phone numbers to steal text-message codes; an authenticator app defeats them.
Password manager, named holders: no shared passwords in a WhatsApp chat; every login attributable to a person.
Same-day offboarding: when someone leaves the team, access dies that day. Not month-end.
Verification & recovery, in peacetime: verified badge, current recovery contacts, platform escalation contacts on file.
Two-person rule for sensitive posts: one drafts, one approves. The account that never misfires is the account with a second pair of eyes.
Personal accounts: yours and your household's
Posting discipline: post the event after you leave the location. No departures, arrivals, convoys, or patterns.
Clean photo data: location tags off, hidden photo data removed, backgrounds checked. A phone photo records where it was taken, to the metre.
Unique passwords and two-step login: one breached shopping site must not open your institutional life.
Assume the friendly stranger: unsolicited recruiters, admirers and “journalists” get verified before they get conversation.
Brief the household: spouses' check-ins and children's school posts map your movements too. The perimeter includes them.

Viral crisis simulation: full facilitator script

Timings · 18 minutes total
T+0. Read the viral post aloud once, verbatim. Start the timer. Tables begin: posture → message → channel → monitoring, in that order.
T+5. Reveal Inject 1 (the photo). Read the blogger's ultimatum aloud. Say only: “The situation has developed.” Do not advise.

A strong model answer, not the only one
Posture: Monitor → Correct, with Escalate running in parallel (unverified claim of a death; checkpoint unit contacted; platform report on the fabricated photo with evidence pack). Message at ~+15 min: holding statement: aware of reports; medical and unit checks underway; no woman reported injured at any checkpoint as of 14:00; update by 15:30. Channel: X first (where the fire is), WhatsApp-formatted card to community leaders, radio brief ready. Monitoring: resharing speed, the influencer, the protest tag, and the photo's spread.
Facilitator notes
The classic failure is over-response: a full rebuttal at +2 to an unverified claim. It collapses at Inject 1. Do not rescue tables mid-inject; the discomfort is the lesson. Watch for a table that answers the blogger privately before posting publicly. Surface it in debrief: whoever you answer first owns your framing.

Appendix A3 · Glossary
29 / 30

Terms used in this session

Misinformation: false content shared by people who believe it; sincere but wrong.
Disinformation: false content created and spread deliberately to deceive.
Malinformation: genuine content deployed out of context to cause harm.
CIB: coordinated inauthentic behaviour: networks of accounts acting in concert while posing as unconnected individuals.
Bot network: automated accounts amplifying content at scale to manufacture apparent consensus.
Sockpuppet: a fake persona operated by a real person, farmed for credibility before use.
Deepfake: AI-generated audio, image or video convincingly imitating a real person or event.
Cheapfake: real media manipulated with simple tools, recaptioned, recut, slowed, or miscontextualised.
Hashtag hijacking: flooding an institution's own campaign tag so its channel delivers the attacker's message.
Pre-bunking / inoculation: building audience resistance before an attack by exposing the technique in advance.
Truth sandwich: correction format. Fact, brief flag of the falsehood, fact again. Never headline the lie.
Holding statement: the first-hour message: aware · acting · when we will speak again.
Geo-metadata / EXIF: location and device data embedded in photos; a selfie's coordinates are accurate to metres.
Credibility bank: trust accumulated through peacetime deposits of helpful, human, truthful content, withdrawn in crisis.
